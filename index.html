What to do when you can't do anything

Or why it's so hard for us to concentrate, especially when we really need to

Why it's so hard to focus

Multitasking

It's especially hard to focus when there are many tasks and they're all important. Where is the legendary multitasking when you (and all the hiring managers of this world) need it so much? The thing is, it simply doesn't exist. Researchers found that the brain struggles to concentrate on even two things at once. And when several important tasks come into focus, the body panics and releases cortisol and adrenaline—"stress hormones." Because of this, we work inattentively: we make mistakes and get tired quickly.

Dopamine

Stress hormones are clear, but it gets even more interesting. Our inability to focus also involves the complete opposite of stress—dopamine. This substance is involved in the brain's reward system. What does it have to do with this? We often get distracted from important tasks by something more "pleasant" for the brain. For example, we check likes on social media. That's when dopamine is released—and we feel pleasure. It becomes a vicious cycle: the more you get distracted, the more pleasure you get.

What reduces concentration?

Multitasking

How can concentration decrease because of... concentration? Any stressful situation (and the rapidly changing world in general) makes our brain constantly "scan" the environment for danger. For example, reading news instead of working. But being focused all the time is impossible. Concentration is a muscle, and it can fail if you don't let it rest.

Food

"Fast carbs"—sugar, white bread, sweets—deliver energy to the brain instantly and help it work better. But this energy leaves as quickly as it arrives. And as a result, we end up on a "carb roller coaster": as soon as the effect of "fast carbs" ends, we feel tired and sleepy. And we want even more fast carbs.

Gadgets

Yes, yes, this is that situation when there's code on the laptop screen, a podcast in the headphones, and a video with funny ducklings playing in the background on the computer. As a result, the brain tries to switch from one task to another—and simply can't. And to return to a state of full concentration, a person needs an average of 20 minutes. So it turns out that the more time we spend on gadgets, the more time we then need to get back to work.

How to concentrate better than a goldfish (that is, longer than three seconds)

5 simple (actually not so simple) tips

Imagine the sky and clouds

Or leaves in a stream. This is about light meditation that helps you calm down. After all, it's often the anxious thoughts about difficult tasks that prevent us from doing those difficult tasks. At such moments, you can close your eyes and imagine that the clear sky is you, and the clouds are anxious thoughts. And no matter how many there are, the clear sky will always be there somewhere, behind the clouds, and won't go away. This exercise will help you return to the present moment, rather than worry about potential problems from the future.

Turn on music

But not just any music, and not your favorite. Your favorite music may be associated with strong positive emotions, which are also very distracting. Quiet, calm, moderately rhythmic music works well. You can even try playing a playlist with "white noise" or neuro-music.

Take a walk

Or even go for a run. It's better to find a park or forest: one experiment showed that contemplating nature increases concentration, while after viewing urban landscapes it decreases. You can combine a walk with physical exercise or running—this way even more oxygen will reach the brain and it will work better. But a simple twenty-minute walk is also a great option.

Eat well

We're not your grandmother, but this is really important. Choose foods that prevent sharp spikes in blood sugar. For example, foods high in fiber: vegetables, berries, legumes (chickpeas, lentils, beans), brown rice, whole grain bread.

Read

Real paper books. This really helps with concentration: to read a social media post, a few minutes or even seconds is enough. But an interesting book can draw you in for several hours and really trains focused attention.
